# Competitive Landscape

Checked 2026-10-16. Claims about other products come from their public docs or listings and are summarized, not tested. Where only secondary sources exist, the row says so.

## Summary

Agent spending guardrails exist in three forms: **vendor policy engines** that decide whether to sign (Coinbase CDP, Turnkey, Crossmint's agent key custody), **on-chain smart account modules** (Safe on EVM, OpenZeppelin smart accounts on Stellar), and **off-chain policy layers** that sit in front of a wallet (PolicyLayer and similar). On Stellar, the building blocks exist (x402 settlement, OpenZeppelin smart accounts with a spending limit policy) and several teams are building tooling around them, but no packaged, x402-ready agent wallet with on-chain limits, approvals, MCP server and owner dashboard was found in production.

The most important competitive signal is from SDF itself: the "x402 on Stellar" announcement describes an x402-MCP server that will chain paid API calls "within user-defined spending policies" as "in active development". Halora should position as the account layer such tools can use, not as a rival MCP server.

## Where Halora is different

1. **Enforced on-chain by the account contract.** Unlike vendor policy engines and off-chain layers, the guarantee does not depend on Halora's servers or the developer's runtime staying uncompromised.
2. **Packaged for agents, not just a primitive.** SDK, MCP server, x402 client and an owner dashboard with approvals, on top of the account. OpenZeppelin accounts and the SCF #44 tooling projects provide primitives and generators; Halora is the product a developer installs.
3. **Policy semantics people understand.** Per-payment limit, per-UTC-day limit, recipients, approval threshold, pause, expiry. No rule selection or policy composition for the user to get wrong.
4. **x402 compatibility treated as a requirement, not an assumption.** The research found concrete compatibility constraints (event checks, expiration windows, signature format) that a generic smart account does not handle out of the box.
5. **Stellar-native economics.** Facilitator-sponsored fees mean the agent never needs XLM; payments cost a fraction of a cent.
